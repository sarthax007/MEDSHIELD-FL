# Clinical Trust Narrative

## Introduction

The goal of this project is to build a highly reliable and clinically trustworthy AI system for medical image analysis using Federated Learning (FL) and Vision Transformers (ViT). As AI systems transition into clinical workflows, doctors must trust the system's decisions. To foster this trust, we are incorporating explainability techniques like Grad-CAM to visualize *why* the model makes a prediction.

## Explainability and Grad-CAM

Grad-CAM (Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping) produces a coarse localization map highlighting the important regions in the medical scan (such as an MRI) that the AI uses to predict the presence of a tumor.

## Current Project Status: Untrained Baseline

> [!WARNING]
> **Primary Limitation: Untrained Baseline**
> Our Vision Transformer (ViT) model is currently initialized with **random weights** and has not yet been trained. Therefore, the heatmaps generated by the system at this stage represent an **untrained baseline** and the model is essentially guessing.

At present, the heatmaps produced by the model (found in `docs/explainability_evidence/`) do not correlate with any true clinical findings or anatomical structures. They highlight random areas of the scan, which perfectly illustrates how a model behaves before acquiring any learned representations.

### Next Steps: Federated Training

Accurate alignment of the Grad-CAM heatmaps with actual tumor regions will **only happen after the federated training phases**. During federated learning, the model will be trained across distributed medical institutions, learning rich feature representations of tumors without compromising patient data privacy.

Once training is complete, the heatmaps will serve as vital clinical evidence, demonstrating that the AI is accurately localizing tumors and focusing on the correct pathological markers rather than learning spurious correlations.
